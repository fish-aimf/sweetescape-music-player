How to use SweetEscape

A complete guide to the player, from adding your first song to the shortcuts that make everyday use faster.

Notes marked Tip cover right-click, long-press and keyboard shortcuts. None of them are required, but they save a lot of clicks once you know the basics. The quick reference at the end lists them all.

To learn inside the player instead, press Alt + Shift + G. Guide mode points at each part of the screen and explains it.

The screen

There are three areas. Music Explorer on the left shows what's playing and what to play next. The tabs in the middle switch between Now Playing, Library, Playlists and Lyrics. The control bar along the bottom has the playback buttons, the progress bar and the volume.

The row of icons at the top right, from left to right:

  • Moon: switches between light, dark and your custom theme (P).
  • Mask: Disguise.
  • Bar chart: Listening statistics.
  • Clock: Sleep timer (T).
  • Discord: Rich Presence. The dot shows whether it's connected.
  • Magnifier: Identify a song.
  • Download arrow: Downloads.
  • Cog: Settings.

Top left is your total listening time and the site's visitor count. Bottom right is the version number, which opens the changelog when clicked.

The two round buttons at the bottom left hide the control bar (H) and move the playback buttons to the left, centre or right of the bar.

Adding songs

Your library starts empty. There are four ways to fill it.

1. Search, then press Enter

Type into Search songs on the Library tab. If nothing in your library matches and you press Enter, it searches YouTube instead. Each result has Preview, which plays it in a small player without adding it, and Add.

The "topic" button inside the search box adds the word "topic" to YouTube searches. Topic uploads are the official audio releases with the album cover as their picture. They load faster and use less data than music videos. It's on when lit and struck through when off.

Tip Paste a YouTube link into the same search box and press Enter to add that exact video.

2. Modify Library

Modify Library, then the Add Song tab, takes a name, an optional artist and a YouTube URL. Paste the URL first and the name fills itself in. Autofill Name & Author splits a title like "Queen - Don't Stop Me Now (Official Video)" into a clean name and artist, and uses the channel name when the title has no artist in it. If you don't like the result, Undo puts back what you'd typed.

Tip Hover over Autofill to preview what it will write before you click.

3. Global library

The Global library button opens a full-screen browser. The rest of the player is hidden until you close it, apart from the control bar, which stays at the bottom so you can keep controlling what's playing; its playlist button opens the side panel over the Global library. On the left is the current Billboard Hot 100 top three, refreshed every day, and Discover Something New, a few random picks with a Refresh button. Click any of them to hear a preview, which pauses whatever was playing until you close it.

The search box on the right finds artists, songs and premade playlists. An artist opens a list of their songs, with Load more at the bottom. A song opens a card with a listen button and an add button. A premade playlist is added to your library in one go, songs and all.

Tip Click the "Billboard Hot 100" heading for the whole chart. Each entry shows its movement since last week, its peak and how long it has charted, with buttons to preview it or add it to your library.

4. Mix Lab

Mix Lab sits under the search box in the Global library. It turns up to five picks into a mix of songs you don't have yet, and plays it straight through like a radio station:

  • Songs from your library: type in the box to search your library, or click it to see your recently played songs and favourites. Favourites, Recently played and Random add a few at once.
  • Moods, genres and artists: type something like "rainy day", "90s R&B" or "songs like Daft Punk" and press Enter, or click one of the suggestions next to Try.

Click Create mix to get up to 24 songs, none of them already in your library, each showing which pick it came from. The round Play button plays the whole mix in the player and Shuffle plays it in random order. Click a song to play the mix from that song. Next, previous, autoplay, the playlist side panel and the miniplayer work as they do for any playlist, and when the mix is nearly over, more songs like it are added so it keeps going.

Hover a song for +, which opens Add Song with the name, artist and link filled in, and ×, which hides it. New picks makes a fresh mix from the same picks.

Import as playlist keeps the whole mix: every song is added to your library and saved as a new playlist named after the mix, in the same order. Songs you already have are reused rather than added twice, and anything that can't be found on YouTube is left out and counted in the message at the end.

Tip A mix you only play isn't saved: it lasts until you play something else, so import it or add the songs you want to keep. In glass mode with album-art backgrounds turned on, the background shows the cover of the song that's playing.

5. Import a list

Modify Library, Import/Export, Import Songs takes plain text, one song per line, and whole playlists in braces. See Sharing and backup for the format.

The library

With the search box empty, the Library tab is a home screen with three shelves:

  • Favourites: every song you've starred. Scroll it sideways with the mouse wheel or a swipe, and click a cover to play it. Play plays them all in order, and All opens them as a list.
  • Playlists: your playlists as cover tiles, plus a New playlist tile. Hover a tile to edit or delete that playlist.
  • From your library: a shuffled grid of your songs with their artists, sized to fit the space: big covers on tall screens, compact tiles on shorter ones. Shuffle deals a new set and Show all shows the full list.

Tip Right-click any cover on these shelves to add the song to the queue instead of playing it straight away.

Searching

Search matches names and artists as you type. When only a few songs match they show as large cards, a few more get small covers, and long lists stay plain so they scroll quickly. Press Enter to play the first match.

It also understands songs in other alphabets typed in Latin letters: eto finds Это, saranghae finds 사랑해, arigatou finds ありがとう, and agapi mou finds Αγάπη μου.

Filters

Hover over the funnel icon in the search box. Each row is Yes, Any or No: Favourite, Lyrics (has lyrics saved) and Downloaded (has a local file). Language works out each song's language from the letters in its title, so you can show only Korean songs, for example. Filters combine with whatever you've typed.

Song rows

Hover over a song in a list to show its buttons: star (favourite), play, edit and delete. Small icons at the end of the row show that it has lyrics, a local file or a star. Clicking the name also plays it.

Tip Right-click a song's play button to queue it.

Tip Drag an audio file from your computer onto a song to play that song from the file from then on.

By default the list is alphabetical with favourites on top. Settings, Additional can switch it to the order you added songs, or reverse it.

Editing a song

The edit (pencil) button opens Edit Song Details:

  • Change the name and artist.
  • The circular arrows next to the URL search YouTube for this song again and let you pick a different video, useful when an upload is a live version or has been taken down.
  • The share button copies the song's /song/… link. See Sharing.
  • Link local file picks an audio file to play instead of YouTube (Chrome and Edge only). The × unlinks it.
  • The lyrics box takes one line per lyric, each ending in a timestamp like [0:33]. Lyrics with no timestamps at all still show, they just don't highlight. Once any line has one, lines without a timestamp are left out.

Playback controls

The control bar, left to right: previous, play/pause, next, loop (repeat this song), autoplay (carry on when the song ends), the list button for the playlist side panel, and speed (0.25x to 2x). Click anywhere on the progress bar to jump there. Volume, speed and loop are remembered for next time. Songs play through YouTube's privacy-enhanced player, which doesn't set advertising or tracking cookies.

Under the song name, Next: tells you what plays after this one, taking the queue and skipped songs into account.

Tip Hover over next or previous to see the three songs on that side.

Tip Right-click the song name in the control bar to copy it.

Now Playing tab

Once a song is playing, a Now Playing tab appears with a large cover and its own controls. While it's open the bottom bar tucks away, since everything it holds is on the tab. Clicking the Now Playing card in Music Explorer also brings you here, and Q cycles through the tabs.

Video mode and the miniplayer

U fills the window with the YouTube video, and pressing U again returns to the player. For Topic uploads that's the album cover.

I pops the player out into a small always-on-top window with the cover and controls, so it stays on screen while you work in another app. This uses Document Picture-in-Picture, which needs Chrome or Edge on a computer.

If you close or reload the tab while music is playing, the browser asks first. Trackpad back swipes, mouse side buttons and Alt + arrow keys are ignored so you don't leave the player by accident.

The queue

The queue holds songs to play next, before the playlist carries on. Press Y to open it.

To add a song, right-click its play button: in a library list, in Music Explorer, in the favourites strip or the song grid. A short note at the bottom of the screen confirms it. Right-clicking the same song twice in a row sets it to play twice rather than listing it twice. Right-click a playlist's cover to queue every song in it.

Inside the queue:

  • The search box adds any library song. Use the arrow keys and Enter to pick one.
  • − and + set how many times a song plays, up to 99.
  • Drag the grip on the left to reorder. Each row also has duplicate and remove buttons.
  • The header buttons shuffle the queue, add a stop block (playback stops when it reaches it), add a loop block (the song before it repeats until you skip), clear it, and close it.

The queue is saved, so it's still there after a reload.

Playlists

With the search box empty, the Playlists tab shows every playlist as a cover tile with its song count. Favorites comes first and keeps itself in sync with your starred songs. The playlist that's playing is outlined.

  • Click a tile to play the playlist. An empty playlist opens in the editor instead, so you can add songs.
  • Hover a tile for two buttons: the pencil opens the editor and the bin deletes the playlist. Deleting always asks first, and the songs stay in your library. Favorites has no bin.
  • New playlist asks for a name, then opens the editor.
  • Drag a tile to reorder your playlists. The shelf in the Library tab follows the same order.

Tip Right-click a cover to add the whole playlist to the queue.

Searching playlists

Type in the search box and the grid turns into a list in up to three groups: playlists whose name matches, playlists that contain a matching song or artist (with the songs named), and artists from your library. Hover a playlist for play, delete and edit. Enter plays the top playlist.

A playlist from an artist

From artist lists every artist in your library with how many of their songs you have, most first. Click one, or the Create playlist button that appears when you hover it, and all their songs go into one playlist named after them, sorted by title.

  • Featured artists count, so Mark Ronson ft. Bruno Mars goes into both artists' playlists. Channel names like ArtistVEVO and Artist - Topic are matched to the artist.
  • Duos and bands stay together: Simon & Garfunkel is one artist unless you also have songs credited to Simon alone.
  • Add more of their songs later and the row offers Add with the number of new songs, which tops up the same playlist even if you've renamed it. Once it's up to date, the button plays it.
  • Artists also appear when you search the Playlists tab, with the same button.

Editing a playlist

The pencil on a playlist opens the playlist editor. Search on the left to add songs (artists match too), drag the handles on the right to reorder, and Remove to take one out.

  • Click the playlist's name at the top to rename it.
  • The circular arrows at the top of the song list shuffle the order permanently.
  • Duplicates Allowed / Banned decides whether the same song can be added twice. Clear Duplicates removes repeats that are already there. Reverse Position flips the order.

Playlist side panel

While a playlist is playing, the list button in the control bar (or M, or Tab) opens its songs on the right. Click a song to jump to it. The search box filters the panel, the green arrows loop the playlist, and the expand arrows switch to a wider panel with covers that moves the page aside instead of covering it.

Tip Long-press a song in the panel to skip it for this session. It's struck through and won't play until you long-press it again or reload. Nothing is removed from the playlist, and if it's the song that's playing, the player moves on straight away.

Music Explorer

The left column. O hides and shows it. From the top:

  • Now Playing: click it to open the Now Playing tab.
  • Recently Listened To: the songs and playlists you played last.
  • Suggested and Your Picks: songs from your library to play next.
  • Recently Played Playlists.

Click a song to play it. Tip Right-click to queue it, click the "Suggested" or "Your Picks" heading for a fresh set, and click "Recently Listened To" for your full history, where you can remove entries and choose how many are kept.

Settings, Recommendation sets how many items each section shows (0 hides it) and the order of the sections.

Lyrics

There are three ways to follow along with a song's lyrics: the Lyrics tab, fullscreen lyrics in five styles, and the karaoke page you can share with someone else.

The Lyrics tab highlights each line as it's sung. The three round buttons in its corner keep the current line centred (or let you scroll freely), copy a karaoke link, and open fullscreen lyrics.

Tip Right-click the Lyrics tab for a list of every song that has lyrics. Copy copies a song's lyrics and Edit opens it in the song editor.

Getting lyrics for a song

When a song has none, the Lyrics tab offers two buttons. Transcribe lyrics opens the Lyrics Maker. Import subtitles as lyrics uses the video's YouTube captions: Auto-Fetch Transcript grabs them (pick a language if there are several), or open the video, copy its transcript and paste it in. Convert to Lyrics turns it into timed lines, drops filler like "[Music]", and lets you tidy the result before saving.

Timing your own lyrics

The Lyrics Maker works in three steps.

  1. Setup: paste the words, or use Auto-Fetch Lyrics (Musixmatch). Buttons beside it search AZLyrics, Letras, Genius and Google for you. Then press Prepare Lyrics.
  2. Record: press Start Recording, then Mark Line the moment each line begins. Redo steps back a line and rewinds the song to match, and Skip moves past a line. Every marked line appears on the timeline above the list, where you can drag its marker to fine-tune the timing.
  3. Export: check the result, then Save to Song or copy it.

Fullscreen lyrics

The expand button on the Lyrics tab asks which style you want, then hides everything except the lyrics, the bottom bar and the visualizer. Esc leaves, and the layers button in the corner changes style without leaving.

Karaoke page

The share button on the Lyrics tab copies a link that holds the song and its timed lyrics. Nothing is uploaded: the lyrics are compressed into the address itself. Whoever opens it gets a karaoke page that follows the same theme as the player.

The page has play/pause, restart, loop, a centring toggle, a speed slider, and a fullscreen lyrics view. Save to Library adds the song, lyrics included, to the visitor's own library, and Back to SweetEscape opens the player.

Sleep timer

The clock icon or T. First choose what happens when it runs out:

  • Stop Music pauses playback.
  • Close App replaces the page with a blank one, so the player isn't left in the tab's back history.

Then pick a preset from 30 seconds to 15 minutes, type any number of minutes, or set a clock time to stop at. The dialog counts down the time left (and the stop time, if you set one), a short countdown also appears next to the clock icon, and Cancel Timer stops it.

Identify a song

The magnifier icon. Choose where the sound comes from:

  • Tab / System audio: share a browser tab or your screen's audio, for a song playing in a video or another app.
  • Microphone: hold your device near the music. Raise Input gain if it's quiet.
  • Upload clip: an audio or video file.

Once there's a recording you can play it back and drag across the waveform to pick the clearest part. Identify selection or Identify clip sends it off. A match shows the cover with links to Shazam, YouTube Music, YouTube and Spotify, and the + button searches for it so you can add it.

Downloads and local files

The download arrow opens a download manager with three tabs: Current song, Search library and YouTube URL. Search library lists every song in your library from A to Z, so you can scroll through all of them or type to narrow the list, with the same multi-language matching as the library search. Songs you have already downloaded, or linked to a file on your device, carry a green Downloaded mark in the list, on the current song and in the queue, so you don't download the same song twice. Add songs to its list, then download them one at a time or with Download all. Finished songs are struck through, and anything in progress can be cancelled. Files are MP3s.

A stand-alone version lives at /download for quick downloads on a phone without opening the player.

To play a downloaded file instead of YouTube, drag it onto the song in your library or use Link local file in the song's edit dialog. Linked songs play with no connection at all, and the visualizer reacts to their actual sound. The player itself also loads offline after your first visit.

Listening statistics

Statistics start once you switch on Track Listening Statistics in Settings, Additional. A song counts as played after you've heard a third of it or ten seconds, whichever is shorter.

The bar chart icon shows the last 30 days or all time: time listened, plays, number of songs, your top artist and your top songs. Click any song for its own history: plays per day or per month, its best day and when you last played it, as a chart or a table.

Switching statistics off deletes everything they've recorded.

Themes and glass

The moon icon (P) cycles light, dark and custom. The browser tab's icon follows your accent colour. Settings, Theme has the rest:

  • Surface Style: Solid, or Glass, which makes panels see-through over a blurred background. Glass has sliders for transparency, blur and vibrancy, plus presets.
  • Background: your own image or one of the built-in gradients, with fit, dim and blur. The image stays on your device.
  • Album Art Background: in glass mode, fills the background with a soft, slowly drifting copy of the current song's cover instead.
  • Visualizer Colour: your theme colour, any colour you pick, or Auto, which takes it from the album cover.
  • Customize Colors (custom theme): set all twelve colours by hand. Export Theme to Clipboard gives you a code to share, Import Theme takes one, and Saved Themes keeps named themes to switch between.

Visualizer

The animation behind the player. Turn it on or off, choose one of six designs (Bars, Levels, Wave, Ribbon, Radial, Glow) and set its size with Magnitude in Settings, Additional. In solid mode it shows at the sides of the screen; in glass mode the panels let you see all of it.

It reads the real audio for songs playing from a local file. YouTube's player doesn't let pages read its sound, so for YouTube songs it moves in time with the music without measuring it.

Disguise

The mask icon. Tab disguise on the left changes the browser tab's icon and title to look like Google Docs, Gmail, Classroom, Desmos and about twenty others. It stays until you choose Original.

Page disguise on the right covers the whole player with a real website, either a preset or any address you type, while the music keeps playing. Esc or the × in the corner brings the player back.

Discord

Shows what you're listening to on your Discord profile. Browsers can't talk to Discord directly, so this uses a small helper program on your computer; the download link is in the dialog. Turn on Enable Rich Presence, and the three status steps on the left turn green once the helper and Discord are connected.

The right side previews what's being sent. You can override the song, artist or picture, Reset to go back to automatic, or Send Now to update straight away.

Sharing and backup

Sharing a song

The share button in a song's edit dialog copies a /song/… link. Pasted into Discord, X or most chat apps it shows a preview card with the cover. Opening it shows a page that plays the song, with a form to add it to your own library.

To share a song together with its timed lyrics, send a karaoke link instead.

Backing up your library

Modify Library, Import/Export. Export Songs can export the library, the library together with every playlist, or a single playlist. The result is plain text you can keep anywhere.

Import Songs takes the same text: one song per line as Name, YouTube URL or Name, YouTube URL, Artist, and playlists in braces:

Road Trip{
Take On Me, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HzdD8kbDzZA, a-ha
Dreams, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5oWyMakvQew, Fleetwood Mac
}

Songs already in your library are skipped, and commas inside song names are handled.

Other settings

  • Recommendation: how many recently played songs to remember, and the order and size of each Music Explorer section.
  • Feedback: send a note to the developer, plus links to this tutorial and the introduction.
  • Additional: ads (off by default), the visualizer, library sort order, and listening statistics.

Guide mode

Press Alt + Shift + G anywhere in the player (Option + Shift + G on a Mac), or open the player with guide mode on.

Guide mode has two parts. The tour dims the screen and steps through each area with a short explanation and its tips. Use the arrow keys or the buttons, and Esc to finish. Point and ask explains whatever you hover over while you keep using the player as normal. On a touchscreen, tap something once to read about it and again to use it.

Guide mode downloads only when you open it, so it adds nothing to the player's loading time.

Quick reference

Every shortcut and gesture from this page, in one list.

Mouse and touch

  • Right-click a song's play button, a Music Explorer song, a favourite or a cover in the song grid: add it to the queue.
  • Right-click a playlist's cover: queue the whole playlist.
  • Long-press a song in the playlist side panel: skip it for this session.
  • Drag a playlist tile: reorder playlists.
  • Hover a playlist: edit or delete it.
  • Click a playlist's name in its editor: rename it.
  • Right-click the Lyrics tab: every song with lyrics.
  • Click a Music Explorer heading: full history (Recently Listened To) or a fresh set (Suggested, Your Picks).
  • Click "Billboard Hot 100" in the Global library: the full chart.
  • Hover next or previous: the three songs on that side.
  • Hover Autofill: preview the cleaned-up name and artist.
  • Right-click the song name in the control bar: copy it.
  • Paste a YouTube link into the library search and press Enter: add it.
  • Drag an audio file onto a song: play it from that file.
  • Click the version number: the changelog.

Keyboard

Y queue, U video mode, I miniplayer, O Music Explorer, Q next tab, F / G seek, T timer. The full list is under Keyboard shortcuts.
