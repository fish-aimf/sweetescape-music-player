SweetEscape is a free music player that runs in your browser.

Search for any song, keep the ones you like in your own library, and build playlists around them. There's no account and nothing to install. Your songs, playlists and settings stay on your device.

Open the player Read the tutorial

What it is

SweetEscape started as a small side project and grew into a full music player. One person builds it, in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and it's updated often. Every change is listed in the changelog.

Songs play through YouTube's embedded player. Your library is a list of songs saved in your browser's own storage (IndexedDB), with their names, artists, lyrics and covers. Nothing is sent to a server. Once you've visited once, the app itself loads without a connection. You can also link a downloaded audio file to any song so it plays straight from your disk.

The catch is that a lot of it is tucked away. Most things can be right-clicked, long-pressed or dragged, and a few features have no button at all, only a key. That's what the tutorial and guide mode are for.

What it can do

Library
Search across names and artists as you type, including Russian, Korean, Japanese and Greek songs typed in Latin letters. Filter by favourite, lyrics, downloaded, or by the language the title is written in. Favourites stay on top.
Finding new songs
Type a song that isn't in your library and press Enter to search YouTube, preview a result, and add it. The Global library shows the current Billboard Hot 100, artist pages, premade playlists and random picks.
Playlists
Drag to reorder, randomize, reverse, allow or ban duplicates. While a playlist plays, its side panel lets you search it, loop it, and long-press a song to skip it just for now.
Queue
Right-click any play button to queue a song. The queue (Y) lets you set how many times each song repeats, reorder by dragging, and drop in "loop forever" or "stop here" blocks.
Lyrics and karaoke
Synced lyrics that highlight as the song plays. Get them from Genius or YouTube subtitles, or time them yourself by tapping along in the Lyrics Maker. Share a karaoke link that carries the lyrics inside the URL.
Fullscreen lyrics
Five styles: Verse, Cards, Mirror, Lyric Video and Flow.
Identify a song
Capture a browser tab or your system audio, use the microphone, or upload a clip. Trim it on the waveform, then open the match in Shazam, YouTube Music, YouTube or Spotify, or add it to your library.
Downloads and local files
Download songs as MP3s, one at a time or as a batch. Drop an audio file onto a song to play it from disk instead of YouTube.
Statistics
Time listened, plays, top songs and top artist for the last 30 days or all time, and a per-day play chart for every song. It's off until you turn it on.
Looks
Light, dark or fully custom colours, saved and shared as theme codes. A glass mode with your own background image or the current album cover. Six visualizer designs.
Everything else
Every shortcut can be rebound, there's a sleep timer, Discord Rich Presence, a picture-in-picture miniplayer, a video mode, playback speed from 0.25x to 2x, a tab disguise, and plain-text import and export of your whole library.

Things most people never find

  • Right-click a song's play button to add it to the queue. This works in the library, Music Explorer, the favourites strip and the song grid. Right-click a playlist's Play button to queue the whole playlist.
  • Press Y to open the queue. It has no button.
  • Long-press a song in the playlist side panel to skip it for this session without removing it.
  • Right-click the Lyrics tab to see every song that has lyrics.
  • Click the "Suggested" or "Your Picks" headings in Music Explorer to reshuffle them, and "Recently Listened To" to see your full history.
  • Hover the next or previous button to see the three songs either side.
  • Paste a YouTube link into the library search and press Enter to add it.
  • Press Alt + Shift + G inside the player for guide mode, which walks you around the screen and explains each part.

A closer look

Where your data lives

Your library, playlists, queue, lyrics, themes and statistics are stored in your browser for this site. There is no sign-in and no copy on a server. That also means clearing your browser's site data wipes it, so use Modify Library, Import/Export now and then to keep a backup. The export is plain text you can read and edit.

When you search, fetch lyrics, identify a song or download one, the request goes to the service that does the work (YouTube, Genius, the Global library database and so on), usually through the site's own small API. Your library itself is never part of those requests.

Questions

Is SweetEscape free?

Yes. There's no paid tier and no account. The site has an optional advertisement setting that is off by default.

Where are my songs and playlists stored?

In your own browser, in IndexedDB. Nothing is uploaded. Clearing site data for sweetescape.vercel.app deletes it, so export your library from Modify Library, Import/Export if you want a backup.

Does it work offline?

The app itself loads offline after your first visit. Songs stream from YouTube, so they need a connection, unless you link a downloaded audio file to the song.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes, it works in mobile browsers. On Android a long press does what right-click does on desktop. A few features, like linking local files and the miniplayer, need desktop Chrome or Edge.

How do I move my library to another computer?

Open Modify Library, go to Import/Export, export your songs (optionally with every playlist), and paste the text into Import Songs on the other computer.

Why doesn't the visualizer react to every song?

The visualizer reads the real audio only for songs playing from a local file. YouTube's player doesn't let a page read its audio, so for those it moves in time with the music instead of analysing it.

Is the source code available?

Yes, on GitHub under the CC BY-NC 4.0 license.

Getting started

  1. Open the player. With an empty library you get a short welcome.
  2. Click Global library and add a few songs, or type one into the search box and press Enter.
  3. Press Alt + Shift + G to have guide mode show you around.
  4. When you want the details, the tutorial goes through every feature with screenshots.
